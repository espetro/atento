There is a particular kind of calm that comes from a full read-later queue. Nothing is lost. Nothing is due. Everything is waiting politely on a shelf you have not looked at in four months.

Saving a link is a tiny contract with your future self. The trouble is that most of these contracts are written in a language your future self does not speak. "Look at this eventually" is not a plan, it is a deferral of guilt.

## What makes a saved link useful

A saved link becomes useful when it has a reason attached to it. Not a category, not a tag, an actual reason. A question you want answered, a decision you are stuck on, a problem you expect to hit again.

The difference is that a link with a reason has an expiry date. You can tell, months later, whether it still matters. A link without a reason just sits there, technically not deleted, functionally gone.

The practical move is small: when you save something, write one sentence about why. If you cannot write the sentence, you probably did not need to save the link.

Once a queue has reasons, it can have an inbox, a triage step, and a sweep. Without reasons, all you have is a slower form of forgetting.
